# Research and references

This section compares documented capabilities, not a usability study. The limitations below describe the scope of the referenced experience relative to Stylora's narrow prototype goal; they are not claims that the platforms lack other capabilities.

## Existing shopping experiences

| Platform | Documented capability | Gap Stylora explores |
|---|---|---|
| Amazon | Personalized recommendations based on signals such as purchase history, Alexa shopping conversations, reviews, Lists, and searches; Amazon also describes generative-AI shopping tools. | Stylora's prototype centers a single explicit request with a budget constraint and produces an outfit combination from the supplied catalog. Its demo data and current service are much narrower than a retail marketplace. |
| ASOS | The ASOS app provides Style Match, which can use photos or screenshots for outfit inspiration. | Stylora explores text-first intent (occasion, style, colour, budget) and a total-budget outfit composition. |

These are adjacent reference points, not direct feature parity claims. Mainstream platforms may also support budgets or outfit discovery; this comparison describes the particular capabilities cited below.

## How Stylora aims to improve the journey

The prototype lets a shopper express multiple constraints in one request, filters candidates against recorded price and availability, and can return a coordinated set whose total remains within budget. It includes short reasons for matches. Since the supplied sample products are simulated, a real improvement in shopping outcomes requires reliable live or curated catalog data and a working frontend/API integration.
